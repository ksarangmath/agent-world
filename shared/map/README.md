# The maps

Agent World has one map per metro (`shared/metros.ts`): the Bay Area at `/` and
Austin at `/austin`. Each is a fictional island that riffs on the real place, on
the same 456 × 504 tile grid. `x` grows east and `y` grows south. A person
belongs to one metro, and nothing crosses between them: places, trips, and
conversations all stay on one map.

On the Bay map, Fog City (San Francisco) is the biggest region: its west side is
squeezed into a compact beach town, the rest is drawn to scale, and everything
farther than 7.5 km from its center is squeezed toward it, so Redwood Reach,
Golden Hills, and Silicon Vale shrink around it. The Austin map does the same
around downtown: Bat City is drawn to scale, and Chill Country, Silicon Hills,
and the Brisket Belt shrink around it. Everything here is seed data plus pure
functions, shared by the Worker and the browser so both always agree.

- **`grid.ts`**: the grid's size, its ground kinds, and `warps`, one
  `warp(lon, lat)` per metro that maps real coordinates onto the grid. It uses
  only arithmetic and `Math.sqrt`, which every JavaScript engine computes
  identically.
- **`<metro>/geography.json`**: generated by `pnpm map <metro>`
  (`scripts/geography.ts`, with each metro's lists in `scripts/sources.ts`) from
  U.S. Census TIGER data: coastline, lakes, and rivers, parks, campuses, the
  city's major streets, main streets and freeways elsewhere, and rail lines, all
  cut to a hand-drawn island outline with a smoothed coast. The ground is stored
  as run lengths per row and the roads as polylines.
- **`<metro>/places.ts`**: the metro's regions and every place, with its
  neighborhood, interest tags, activity, real location, and capacity.
  **`places.ts`** joins them: `places` and `placesById` cover every metro (place
  ids are unique across all of them), and `placesIn(metro)` is one metro's list.
- **`<metro>/transit.ts`**: the stations, the rides between them (rail and
  ferries, each linking two stations), and where new people arrive. Rides
  follow real lines and real stations: BART, Caltrain, and the ferries in the
  Bay, and the one commuter rail line in Austin (Downtown, Highland,
  Kramer by the Domain, and Lakeline). Everywhere else people walk the roads.
- **`terrain.ts`**, **`route.ts`**, **`spots.ts`**: decode the ground,
  rasterize the roads and route over them, and claim each place's spots. A route
  walks to the nearest road tile reachable from the arrival station, takes the
  quickest path over roads and rides, and walks to the destination. Every ride
  adds a fixed boarding time. The search is deterministic, with ties broken by
  tile index, so the server's arrival time and the browser's animation match. A
  place's spots grow outward from its location over free, dry ground off the
  roads, keeping a one-tile gap from other places. Agents stand on spots, and a
  unique index on (place, tile) in the database enforces capacity.
- **`atlas.ts`**: `atlasOf(metro)` builds all of the above for one metro, once,
  the first time it's asked for.

To add a place, add a row with its real latitude and longitude. `map.test.ts`
checks, for every metro, that spots are on dry ground, off the roads, and not
shared, and that every place is reachable. Moving a map or a place changes spot
tiles, so people already standing on old tiles must be moved to their place's
new spots.

To add a metro, add it to `shared/metros.ts`, give it a projection in `grid.ts`,
lists in `scripts/sources.ts`, and a folder here, then follow the type errors:
each per-metro table in the Worker and in `web/map` is a `Record<Metro, …>`.
