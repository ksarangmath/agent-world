# The map view

A toon-shaded three.js scene, rendered in fine pixels with
`RenderPixelatedPass` (`pixel.tsx`, which also quantizes and grades color,
blurs the edges of the screen more the closer you zoom, and makes
lights glow after dark, each in its own color) and seen from the south-southwest through an
orthographic camera. One world unit is one tile. Nothing here talks to the server except
the world snapshot that `People` and `Labels` draw.

- **`elevation.ts`**: named hills raise a height grid under the land, and the
  distance from shore shades the water.
- **`decks.ts`**: every bridge deck as slabs, each a sloped plank between two
  points. A deck rises from the ground where its road meets land to its
  span's height; the three big spans follow the centerline of their own road
  pieces. `landmarks/bridgeworks.ts` draws these slabs and `surfaceAt` puts
  walkers and traffic on the same ones, so nothing floats above a bridge or
  sinks into it. **`lines.ts`** joins road lines end to end, for traffic and
  for finding where a road crosses water.
- **`city.ts`**: derives the city from the ground and roads. Each tile takes
  the style of its nearest neighborhood (`neighborhoods.ts`): Victorian rows in
  the Mission, stucco in the Sunset, towers downtown, warehouses in Dogpatch,
  houses along the suburbs' main streets. Each block is split into a few big
  lots, one building each; parks, courtyards, and wild hills get trees.
- **`ground.tsx`**, **`water.tsx`**, **`buildings.tsx`**, **`trees.tsx`**: draw it.
  The ground, buildings, and trees come in 64-tile chunks so off-screen chunks
  are culled. A ground shader paints lane lines and curbs up close and
  drifting cloud shadows, and lights the streets after dark; windows,
  shopfronts, and roof lights come from the wall shader and light up at night
  in the colors `paint.ts` shares (`nightLights`).
- **`landmarks.tsx`**: bridges and the Bay Area's icons. Its `reservedTiles`
  keep procedural buildings off their footprints.
- **`traffic.tsx`**, **`fog.tsx`**: cars, cable cars, streetcars, boats,
  trains, planes, gulls, and the fog that rolls in from the ocean mornings and
  evenings, all derived from the wall clock so every viewer sees the same
  thing.
- **`places.tsx`**, **`people.tsx`**, **`labels.tsx`**: the props at each
  place, the agents walking, riding, doing their place's activity or talking,
  and the HTML labels for regions, neighborhoods, and places. A place's label
  is a button that shows how many people are there and flies the camera to
  them; zoomed out it shrinks to the count, and counts that would overlap
  merge into the busiest place's.
  `routing.worker.ts` computes travelers' routes off the main thread.
- **`sprites.tsx`**, **`lpc.ts`**, **`lpc.json`**: people as Liberated Pixel
  Cup sprites. `lpc.ts` turns an avatar into layers (sheet, depth, palette
  rows), and `sprites.tsx` draws one instanced billboard per sheet, recoloring
  in the shader. `lpc.json` is generated by `pnpm lpc`.
- **`talks.tsx`**, **`hover.ts`**, **`PersonHover.tsx`**: one animated speech
  bubble over each talking pair that opens the conversation when clicked, and
  the card shown when hovering over a person.
- **`sightlines.ts`**: a per-tile height above which buildings, trees, and
  decorations would hide a place's spots from the camera; their shaders dither
  those fragments away when zoomed in.
- **`sky.tsx`**: lighting from the real sun height over San Francisco, with
  the sun at a fixed bearing so shadows fall toward the camera, and moonlight
  at night. **`paint.ts`** holds the shared toon material.
  **`clock.ts`** shares the time, daylight, the visible area (set once a
  frame by `camera.tsx`), so moving things skip work off screen, and whether
  the view is zoomed in past `detailZoom`. Zoomed out, only buildings and
  landmarks cast shadows and outlines come from depth alone, which drops about
  half the draw calls.
- **`camera.tsx`** also runs the frame loop: 30 frames a second, and the
  display's full rate while the camera moves.
- **`ambient.tsx`**: after a minute without input, glides the camera between
  live conversations and busy places until someone touches anything.
- **`quality.ts`**: the quality levels and when to move between them.
  `pixel.tsx` times one whole frame a second. Three slow ones in a row step
  down a level: no normals pass, then art pixels twice as big, then no shadows
  (`sky.tsx`), then no glow. The blur stays at every level. Five good seconds
  step back up, after a wait that doubles each time a step up doesn't hold, so
  a machine that was busy for a moment gets the full picture back.
